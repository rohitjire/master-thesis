1. Critical

Critical

The thesis promises four connectors and delivers three

Chapters 1–4 state throughout that the study benchmarks four implementations, naming Tractus-X EDC among them. Chapter 5 then excludes Tractus-X — "could not be brought to instrumentation parity … within the time frame of this work" — and Chapters 5, 6 and 8 report three. The exclusion is acknowledged only in Ch. 5 and the Ch. 6 threats section. An examiner reads the abstract and Chapter 1 first, and will hit this contradiction on page one.

Unreconciled locations: abstract.tex:5, 01:18, 01:27, 03:68 and the "This thesis" row of tab:related-work-comparison, 04:8, 04:14, 04:86 (goal G1 environment), 04:129 (figure caption: "generalised across the four connectors"), plus roughly ten "four implementations" references in Ch. 2.

Fix: decide the framing and apply it once, everywhere. The defensible version keeps four in Ch. 2 (four implementations exist and are profiled) but states three in the abstract, the objective, the Ch. 3 table row, and the Ch. 4 goal — with a single forward sentence in Ch. 1 noting that Tractus-X is profiled but not instrumented, and why. Don't leave the reader to discover it in Chapter 5.

Critical

The Statutory Declaration page is empty

main.tex:339–345 sets \thispagestyle{empty} and a PDF bookmark named "Statutory Declaration" — and then the document ends. There is no declaration text. For a TU Dortmund master's thesis the Eigenständigkeitserklärung is mandatory, and a missing one is a formal defect independent of the thesis content.

Fix: paste your faculty's exact required wording (do not paraphrase it — the wording is prescribed), with place, date, and signature line. While you're there: check whether your examination regulations require an AI-use declaration. You have used AI substantively for drafting and LaTeX support; most German programmes now permit that if declared, and declaring is the cheap path where concealing is the expensive one.

Critical

The abstract is still a proposal, not a thesis abstract

preamble/abstract.tex is written in the future tense throughout — "This thesis aims to conduct", "the thesis will also analyze", "The research will adapt", "The results … will contribute" — and states no findings at all. The work is finished; Chapter 6 has the numbers. It also says "four connector implementations" (see above), opens a sentence with "Furthermore", promises "valuable insights" and "areas for improvement" without specifics, and uses US spellings (organizations, analyze, utilization) where the entire body uses British -ise/-isation — 25 instances of "utilisation" alone.

It also never mentions X1, the measured cost of identity, which is the thesis's headline contribution.

Fix: rewrite in past/present to match the finished work, name three connectors, and state the actual results — the measured identity overhead, the capacity finding, and the fact that the host was never the limit. An empirical abstract that contains no numbers is the single most-read page of the thesis working against you. This is the highest-value hour you can spend.

2. Warnings

Warning

Two different k6 versions across the campaign, never discussed

05:62 records the load generator as k6 v2.0.0 (v2.2.0 for the endurance runs). That string is the only mention in the thesis — the version change appears in no parity argument, no threats-to-validity item, and no reproducibility note. It matters because the endurance run feeds the RQ3 resource comparison in §6.3, and the whole study rests on holding every factor constant except identity. Chapter 5 even argues the k6 driver is "byte-identical across all connector repositories".

Fix: one sentence in §6.7 stating that the endurance scenario ran on v2.2.0, that the same version was used for both arms of every connector (confirm this from your run folders), and therefore that it cannot bias the G1−G2 difference. If it was not uniform across arms, that is a genuine limitation and needs saying.
