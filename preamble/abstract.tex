\subsection*{Abstract}

Dataspace connectors implement the Dataspace Protocol (DSP) and mediate every cross-organisational data exchange in a dataspace, yet they are compared on features and protocol compliance far more often than on performance. Where performance evidence exists, it rarely holds the identity and trust layer constant.

This thesis benchmarked three DSP-conformant connectors (the Fraunhofer ISST EDC, the Factory-X EDC, and DSP-Native BaSyx) on a normalised single-host substrate under a Goal--Question--Metric design. Each connector was deployed twice, identically in every layer except one: once with its full identity stack running, and once with identity verification mocked, so that the difference between the two deployments measures the cost of identity directly.

The two EDC-derived connectors performed alike at a realistic operating point, and DSP-Native BaSyx completed the same transaction in roughly three fifths of their time. Identity added a fixed cost per request, confined to the control plane and independent of the work the request performed: 30 to 45\,ms of provider-side latency per transaction. The asynchronous protocol, rather than the identity layer, dominated end-to-end time in all three connectors, accounting for between 93 and 97 per cent of it, and each connector reached its throughput ceiling well before the host was exhausted. Alongside these findings this thesis contributes the benchmark harness and the analysis pipeline that produced them.
