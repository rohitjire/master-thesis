\subsection*{Abstract}

The Dataspace ecosystem is growing as a reliable solution for organizations to securely and sovereignly share data within a decentralized framework, with connectors being the backbone of this system. However, performance analysis and benchmarking for these systems is still a gap. This thesis aims to conduct a systematic performance benchmark, focusing on four dataspace connector implementations -- three derived from the Eclipse Dataspace Components (EDC) and one (DSP-Native BaSyx) built on a different framework. Furthermore, the thesis will also analyze the results to identify bottlenecks and areas for improvement in connector implementations, providing insights for future research and development in this field.

The research will adapt the GQM (Goal--Question--Metric) approach to define the performance goals, formulate research questions, and identify relevant metrics for evaluation. The study will involve setting up a controlled environment to conduct experiments and collect data on various performance aspects of these connectors, such as latency, throughput, resource utilization, and scalability under different workloads and configurations.

The results of this thesis will contribute to the understanding of the performance characteristics of dataspace connectors and provide valuable insights for practitioners and researchers in the field of data exchange and integration.
